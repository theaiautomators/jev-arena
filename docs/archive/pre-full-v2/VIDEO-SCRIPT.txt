So what is a decision model?

Imagine a customer sends a message about a duplicate charge. You need to send it to Billing, Engineering, Customer Success or Review.

You might also need a yes-or-no judgment about urgency, or a score on a defined scale.

A normal language model can generate those answers. A decision model is designed to choose among specified outcomes, often returning scores or probabilities directly.

It can still choose the wrong outcome. And a high confidence number can still be wrong.

Jev is TypeSafe's hosted service. I'm testing a pinned version through its API.

Now let's go back to the workflows.

The warehouse is deliberately small. Every seed still has an open route around the outside edge. This is a basic constraint test, not a difficult navigation benchmark.

The model sees all four direction choices. Impossible actions are not masked out.

When a deterministic model makes a blocked move, the state stays the same. It can keep returning that same bad answer. Seventy violation steps can therefore come from a couple of repeated mistakes, rather than seventy independent failures.

In a production system, I would consider validating actions or removing impossible choices. That would be a different system to test.

The ticket results have another trap.

Several models sent every ticket to Review. That avoided violating the rule about restricted accounts, but it sent sixteen ordinary tickets to Review unnecessarily. They got eight of the twenty-four routes right.
